Poles are dry · quick guide

Read short answers correctly

  • "ok" / "jasne" / "dzięki" = got it. Ask the next question.
  • "zobaczę" / "pomyślę" = real "I'll think". Give a date: "To odezwę się w czwartek, ok?"
  • "napiszę" = often he won't. Follow up on day 2 without guilt.
  • "nie, dzięki" = a clear no. "Jasne, powodzenia." and close.
  • He writes "Pan" = mirror it.
  • Short, direct, max 1 emoji. Don't apologise for writing. Facts beat promises.
  • Insist: no reply → days 2, 7, 14, then stop. After a call: 24 h, 48 h, day 5.

Closing call · 35–40 min

Connect, listen, summarise, propose, close.

Fixed order. You adapt the tone to the person, never the order. You speak 30 % of the time at most. Lines in boxes are almost literal: say them in your words, but don't skip any. Polish business owners value directness: short questions, clear numbers, no hype. A quiet, serious prospect isn't a cold one.

Before the call (5 min of prep, mandatory)

  • ☐ Read his CRM card: origin (DM, ad, website, store, referral, former client) and what he wrote.
  • ☐ If he sent labs: look at testosterone (total/free, SHBG), estradiol, fasting glucose/insulin, cortisol, vitamin D, lipids. Note one line to mention.
  • ☐ Mark him cold, warm or hot, and write down one literal sentence of his for the summary.
  • ☐ Note his job, hours a week and family situation (kids, travel).
  • ☐ Have the most similar case ready (only real and with permission) and your story in 30 seconds.
  • ☐ Check the current price level in the CRM (Clients tab). Prices in złoty.
  • ☐ Payment link / bank details ready; ask if he wants a company invoice (NIP).
  • ☐ Camera at eye level, your light on, phone silent. Start on the minute — punctuality is respect.
Entry A · New lead
  • "Good to meet you. Where are you calling from — office or home?"
  • "You wrote RESET after the reel about [topic]. What made you write?"
  • From the store: "We spoke at the counter about energy. What made you book the call?"
  • Referral: "[Name] told me about you. What did he tell you about how we worked?"

Note: keep small talk short. Most Polish business owners prefer getting to the point; two minutes is enough.

Entry B · Former client
  • "Good to talk again. How have you been since we stopped working together?"
  • "Did you keep training? What happened to what we'd achieved?"
  • "When we stopped, what happened?" (listen without defending yourself)

He already trusts you: discovery is shorter. What changes is telling him what you do differently now: bloodwork first, a plan built around his calendar, weekly check-ins, retests. If he reached his goal, also ask for permission, photos and a sentence for a case study.

Phase 1 · Connection + Authority (3–5 min)

He relaxes and understands that you lead the call.

Authority frame (mandatory, almost literal)

"Here's how this works. I'll ask you some questions to understand where you are — work, body, energy, and your labs if you have them. If I see I can really help, I'll explain how I work and we'll see if it makes sense. If I see it's not for you, I'll tell you that too. Okay?"

Wait for the yes: it's the first commitment of the call. Mistakes to avoid: talking about yourself or your medals before you know anything about him, or explaining the programme too early.

Phase 2 · Deep discovery (20 min)

This is where the sale is won or lost. Ask, listen, write down his exact words, hold the silences. No solutions yet.

2.1 Pain

"To start: why did you decide to have this call now and not six months ago?"

  • What does a normal week look like? Hours at work, travel, kids, sleep?
  • How's your energy across the day? When does it drop?
  • How do you feel about your body today compared to five years ago?
  • Give me a recent moment when you noticed it: a photo, a holiday, a meeting where you couldn't focus, stairs…

Silence of 5–7 seconds after each answer. Polish men often answer briefly ("normalnie", "jakoś leci"): that's not resistance. If he stays on the surface:

"I'm asking because the better I understand what's going on, the better I can help. Take your time."

If he's still on the surface, use your story (under 30 seconds):

"Let me tell you why I ask. I started at 64 kilos at 180 centimetres, at twelve. Then I became a bodybuilder and did everything extreme — six forced meals a day, shortcuts I wouldn't recommend to anyone. It got me medals and almost cost me everything else. Today I run a business and train three times a week, and I feel better than ever. What changed wasn't motivation. It was a system that fits a full calendar. Where do you see yourself in that?"

Keep one main pain and write it in the CRM in his words.

2.2 Attempts and labs

"I'm sure you've tried before. Tell me about one or two serious attempts and what didn't work."

  • What exactly failed? How long did it last? Why did you stop?
  • Have you done blood tests in the last year? Did anyone explain them to you?
  • Any medication or anything hormone-related I should know about?

Listen for the pattern: crash diets, training 6 days then nothing, a gym plan that ignored his calendar, nobody ever read his labs. That's your bridge to the method.

2.3 Fears and risk

"Imagine you start a programme like this today — not necessarily mine. What fear or doubt would come to mind?"

  • Have you worked with a coach before? How did it go?
  • What could make you drop it halfway?

Write the literal fear ("I don't have time", "I travel a lot", "I've tried everything", "I don't want to give up my social life"). You solve it in phase 3.

2.4 Desire and ideal service

"What would have to happen in 3 months and in 6 months for you to say: this is working?"

  • And when you get there, what changes? (energy for the kids in the evening, focus in the afternoon meetings, wearing a shirt with confidence, being an example for the team…)
  • Why is this important now?
  • From 1 to 10, how important is it to solve it now?

"If you were to hire someone for this, what one or two things would matter most to you?"

If the 1–10 is under 7: "What would it take to make it a 9?" If there's no real urgency, don't force the close.

Phase 2.5 · Summary + Validation (2 min)

"Let me check I got it right. You [job / hours], and [pain in his words]. You tried [attempt] and it didn't work because [reason]. What you really want is [desire], and what worries you most is [fear]. Am I missing anything important?"

"I'll be straight with you. I believe I can help, and what you're describing is very common among the people I coach. You've sorted the business — your body and energy are the last debt. You don't need more willpower; you need a plan built on your labs that fits your calendar. I'll explain how in three minutes and then you tell me what questions you have. Okay?"

If you see it's not for him (wants a shortcut without labs, wants a stage, won't do check-ins, haggles from the start), say it here.

Phase 3 · Proposal + Price (5 min)

You don't explain the whole service. Only what solves HIS pain and HIS fear.

What it is (20 seconds)

"I help busy professionals and business owners over 30 settle the one debt they keep postponing — their body and their energy — with a plan read from their own bloodwork that fits a full calendar. 500+ transformations, ten years of experience, and a past as a bodybuilder, so I know what not to do."

Block close

"That's the important part. Now tell me: what questions do you have — about how it works, the payment or when to start?"

  1. Clarify: "What exactly do you mean?"
  2. Isolate: "Leaving that aside, is there anything else stopping you from starting today?"
  3. Solve: validate what he feels and give a concrete way out.

Buying signals

If he asks "how do I pay?", "when do we start?", "where do I do the labs?" — stop selling and move to payment.

Payment

"Perfect. I'll send you the payment link now and we'll stay on for a minute until the confirmation comes through. Invoice to the company or personal?"

Don't hang up until payment is confirmed. Transfer, BLIK or Przelewy24 link; invoice with his NIP if he wants it.

If he doesn't show up

  • After 5 minutes: "[Imię], czekam na rozmowie. Coś Ci wypadło? Możemy przełożyć na jutro." (EN: I'm waiting on the call. Did something come up? We can move it to tomorrow.)
  • If he answers, reschedule once, within 1–2 days.
  • If not, back to follow-ups (days 2, 7, 14).

If he doesn't close on the call

  • 24 h: voice note recalling his goal in his words.
  • 48 h: the most similar case (with permission): "this is what I meant".
  • Day 5: last message with a concrete date to decide.
  • Leave him in the CRM as "Call done" with his pain and objection in his words.

Post-close · setting (the first 24 h decide if he arrives motivated or doubtful)

  • ☐ Welcome message the same day (voice note is best).
  • ☐ Send the lab list, the intake form (job, hours, sleep, meds, injuries) and the onboarding guides.
  • ☐ Book the lab review call for when results arrive.
  • ☐ Written permission for photos/messages (with or without face) and separate consent for health data.
  • ☐ Move him to "Client" in the CRM and raise the price level.
Before every call, remember
  • Silence works for you.
  • The order doesn't break.
  • You don't have to convince anyone. If it's for him, he'll see it.
  • Talk like a colleague who's been there, not like a salesman. Short sentences, clear numbers.
  • 500+ transformations and a bodybuilder's past: you already have the authority. Use it quietly.
If he stops replying

Follow-up sequence

  • Day 0: last message of the conversation.
  • Day 2: "[Imię], podbijam wiadomość. Bez presji — po prostu ciekawi mnie, jak to u Ciebie wygląda."
  • Day 7: a piece of content or case that fits what he wrote.
  • Day 14: close: "Piszę ostatni raz… napisz RESET."
  • If he gave you his phone: save it so he sees your WhatsApp Status.
  • Then to the reactivation list (30 days).
